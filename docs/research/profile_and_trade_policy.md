### A3. Development phases (pick 5–6)

| # | Period | One-line description | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1819–1950s: entrepôt port | Raffles founded a free port in Feb 1819 (no port dues; duties on only a few goods). It became a regional entrepôt within 5 years, and the economy rested on re-export trade | NLB Port of Singapore: https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_2018-04-20_085007.html ; 1819 Treaty: https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_2014-05-16_133354.html |
| 2 | 1961–1970s: labour-intensive industrialisation | EDB was set up on **1 Aug 1961**. It first pursued import substitution with labour-intensive factories (garments, toys, wood products, wigs), then turned to export-oriented manufacturing led by MNCs after independence on **9 Aug 1965**. Jurong Industrial Estate was the base, and full employment was reached by the late 1970s | NLB EDB: https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_2018-01-08_135544.html (via search snippet); NLB separation: https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_2020-08-03_151233.html ; Roots.gov.sg: https://www.roots.gov.sg/stories-landing/stories/transforming-landscapes-improving-lives-50-years-of-economic-development/story |
| 3 | 1979–84: "Second Industrial Revolution" | The National Wages Council pursued a **wage-correction policy (1979)**: deliberately high wage rises to push firms out of cheap labour into higher value-added work. The Skills Development Fund was set up in Oct 1979 under the Skills Development Levy Act | NLB SDF: https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=98e1b55f-093d-4d44-b219-d51f6a38c313 ; Cambridge chapter on the high-wage policy: https://cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/CBO9789814379373A013/type/BOOK_PART |
| 4 | 1985–86: first recession and its response | 1985 was the first post-independence recession (Roots: −1.7% for the year; NLB: −1.4% in Q2 and −3.5% in Q3). Causes cited include high costs, falling oil prices and a world trade slump. The Economic Committee chaired by Lee Hsien Loong reported in **Feb 1986** (*The Singapore Economy: New Directions*). The response cut corporate tax and CPF and restrained wages, and made **services a second engine of growth** alongside manufacturing | NLB recession: https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=9f9489cf-5432-4797-bf66-fd1b3bab7a2b ; Roots (link above) |
| 5 | 1990s: regionalisation and capital-intensive growth | The Strategic Economic Plan (Oct 1991) set a 20–30-year blueprint built on industry clusters and the "growth triangle". The regionalisation drive followed, including the **China–Singapore Suzhou Industrial Park (1994)** | NLB SIP: https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_2015-02-17_101058.html ; NLB SEP book record: https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/book-detail?cmsuuid=b37464f7-83ef-4fac-a098-cc1566548fee |
| 6 | 2000s: knowledge economy | The **Biomedical Sciences Initiative (2000)** made BMS the "fourth pillar" next to electronics, engineering and chemicals. **Biopolis** opened in 2003. The Economic Review Committee (2001–03) responded to the 2001 recession | A*STAR: https://research.a-star.edu.sg/articles/features/biopolis-ten-years-on/ ; MTI economic reviews: https://www.meti.gov.sg/industry/economic-reviews/ |
| 7 | 2010s: productivity-led growth | The Economic Strategies Committee (2009–10) and **Budget 2010** set a target of **2–3% productivity growth a year** for a decade, backed by S$5.5bn and the National Productivity and Continuing Education Council. The Committee on the Future Economy (2016–17) led to **23 Industry Transformation Maps** covering about 80% of GDP | MOF Budget 2010: https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/minister-for-finance-mr-tharman-shanmugaratnam-delivered-the-2010-budget-statement-announcing-initiatives-to-raise-productivity-grow-globally-competit/ ; METI reviews (above) |
| 8 | 2020s: resilience, digital/AI and energy | The Emerging Stronger Taskforce (2020–21) followed COVID. The **Economic Strategy Review** set out 32 recommendations on 13 May 2026, with the final report in late Jun 2026 (gov.sg). Its themes are AI, workforce adaptation and new growth engines. MTI was renamed METI on 1 Oct 2026 to put energy at the centre | METI reviews (above); gov.sg ESR: https://www.gov.sg/features/esr/final-report/ ; Allen & Gledhill: https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33280/economic-strategy-review-final-report-published |

### A5. PISA

- **PISA 2022 (released 5 Dec 2023):** Singapore ranked **1st of 81 systems in mathematics, reading and science**. Source: MOE: https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/press-releases/20231205-singapore-strong-showing-in-pisa-2022-affirms-resilience-of-education-system-through-covid-19-pandemic . Mean scores were **maths 575, reading 543, science 561**, as reported by the press and consistent across outlets. The OECD country note returned 403, so treat the exact scores as secondary-sourced. OECD note: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_ed6fbcc5-en/singapore_2f72624e-en.html
- **PISA 2025 (newer; MOE release 8 Sep 2026):** Singapore was **1st in reading (535)**, **2nd in maths (563)** and **2nd in science (560)**; B-S-J-Z (China) led maths (612) and science (597). In Computational Problem Solving Singapore scored 563 (2nd; Macao 572). Comparators: Hong Kong R480 / M522 / S496; Malaysia R393 / M397 / S419; Viet Nam R392 / M443 / S457. OECD averages: R461 / M463 / S482. Source: MOE release https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/press-releases/20260908-pisa-2025-singapore-students-demonstrate-strong-real-world-problem-solving-skills-in-computational-thinking-domain and its Annex A PDF https://www.moe.gov.sg/media/newsroom/news/press/2026/c483088f-19b8-4bf3-8014-3591d4cc44b9.pdf

### B8. ASEAN integration stage and the Balassa ladder

- **AFTA** was launched 28 Jan 1992 through the CEPT scheme (NUS CIL link above). **ATIGA** has been in force since 17 May 2010 (WTO RTA).
- **AEC** was formally established **31 Dec 2015**. **AEC Blueprint 2025** was adopted 22 Nov 2015 at the 27th ASEAN Summit in Kuala Lumpur and covers 2016–2025. Sources: https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/AECBP_2025r_FINAL.pdf ; AEC fact sheet https://www.asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/56.-December-2015-Fact-Sheet-on-ASEAN-Economic-Community-AEC-1.pdf
- **ASEAN Community Vision 2045** ("ASEAN 2045: Our Shared Future") and the Kuala Lumpur Declaration on ASEAN 2045 were adopted **26 May 2025** at the 46th ASEAN Summit. The **AEC Strategic Plan 2026–2030** (6 goals, 192 measures) was endorsed by the 25th AECC on 25 May 2025. Sources: https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ASEAN-2045-Our-Shared-Future.pdf ; https://www.mti.gov.sg/Newsroom/Press-Releases/2025/05/ASEAN-Charts-Strategic-Course-For-Deeper-Regional-Integration-And-Global-Partnerships-At-25th-AECC
- **RCEP** has been in force since 1 Jan 2022 and fully in force for all 15 parties since 2 Jun 2023 (ESG rcep page).
- **Balassa classification** (my analytical reading, not a quoted source):
  - ASEAN sits at the **free trade area** stage. Each member keeps its own external tariff, and Singapore's MFN applied average is 0.0% while its neighbours' are higher, so there is **no common external tariff and no customs union**.
  - The 2007 AEC Blueprint aims at a "single market and production base" with free flow of goods, services, investment and skilled labour, and freer capital (https://www.asean.org/wp-content/uploads/images/archive/5187-10.pdf). Those are common-market-like ambitions layered on top of an FTA.
  - RCEP and CPTPP are also FTAs.

### B10. Non-tariff measures

- **WITS (UNCTAD TRAINS data), Singapore NTM summary**:
  - Imports: coverage ratio **39.30%**, frequency ratio **27.79%**. Exports: coverage 18.94%, frequency 15.54%.
  - **1,343** import products are NTM-affected.
  - Top measures are all **TBT (chapter B)**: B140 authorisation for TBT reasons, B310 labelling, B420 transport and storage, B820 testing, B840 inspection. Then F650 import licence fee and E119 licensing.
  - Sectors with the highest coverage: fuels 99.8%, animal 99.2%, vegetable 96.7%, food products 95.4% (SPS-heavy).
  - The data year is not shown on the page.
  - Source: https://wits.worldbank.org/tariff/non-tariff-measures/en/country/SGP
- ERIA NTM update (2019): NTM-related regulations fell from 90 (2015) to 88 (2018). ERIA describes the regime as decentralised and "non-protectionist", centred on TBT rather than SPS or quotas. Sources: https://www.eria.org/uploads/media/15.ERIA_Book_2019_NTM_Update_Chapter_8.pdf ; https://www.eria.org/RPR_FY2015_No.1_Chapter_10.pdf
- WTO QR notifications: 7 notifications (latest G/MA/QR/N/SGP/7, 1 Sep 2024) covering about 20 export QRs, e.g. non-automatic licensing of strategic goods, rubber and hazardous substances. Source: https://qr.wto.org/en/profile/singapore
- Latest WTO Trade Policy Review on that profile is WT/TPR/S/413/Rev.1 (the 2021 review). A newer TPR could not be confirmed.

### B11. Zones, hubs and incentives

- **Free Trade Zones** (Free Trade Zones Act). Since **1 Mar 2024**, FTZ operators need a Singapore Customs **FTZ Operator Licence**.
  - **Sea FTZs**: Keppel (incl. Keppel Distripark and Brani Terminal, operated by PSA), Pasir Panjang, Jurong, Tuas, Sembawang.
  - **Air FTZs**: Changi FTZ, Airport Logistics Park of Singapore (ALPS), Changi Airfreight Centre, Air Cargo Express.
  - Sources: Customs licence page https://www.customs.gov.sg/permits-and-licences/industry-and-commodity-schemes/free-trade-zone-operator-license/ ; zone list from the Customs FAQ https://ask.gov.sg/customs/questions/cm7lx1tga01iwlrwzooljbwqo . I read the FAQ only via search snippet; the page returned 403, so **verify the list**. Regime change: https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2024/april-24th/singapore-the-revised-free-trade-zones-regime.html
- **Licensed Warehouse scheme**: dutiable goods stored with duty and GST suspended. **Zero-GST Warehouse scheme**: non-dutiable goods, GST suspended. **Major Exporter Scheme** (IRAS): GST suspension on imports for firms that re-export substantially. Sources: https://www.customs.gov.sg/businesses/importing-goods/import-procedures/depositing-goods-in-licensed-zerogstwarehouses/ ; https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-%28gst%29/general-gst-schemes/major-exporter-scheme-%28mes%29
- **Jurong Island**: seven southern islands amalgamated by reclamation (about 3,000 ha), completed 2009, 25th anniversary in 2025. Over 100 energy and chemicals firms (ExxonMobil, BASF, Sumitomo). "Sustainable Jurong Island" plan from 2021. Sources: https://www.jtc.gov.sg/jurongisland/about ; https://www.jtc.gov.sg/about-jtc/news-and-stories/press-releases/jurong-island-celebrates-25-years
- **one-north**: JTC R&D estate (Biopolis 2003, Fusionopolis). Named among JTC's key estates at https://www.jtc.gov.sg/ ; Biopolis date from A*STAR (A3 row 6). A detailed JTC one-north page was not read.
- **Tuas Port**: officially opened 1 Sep 2022. Phase 1 (21 berths, 20m TEU) is due 2027. Full capacity of **65m TEU** in the **2040s**, which will make it the world's largest fully automated port. Sources: MOT https://www.mot.gov.sg/what-we-do/maritime/Shaping-the-Future-of-Maritime-Singapore ; MPA https://mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/port-of-the-future
- **EDB tax incentives**:
  - **Pioneer Certificate (PC)**: tax exemption or a 5% concessionary rate for "pioneering" activities not yet carried out in Singapore.
  - **Development and Expansion Incentive (DEI)**: concessionary 5%, 10% or 15% (EDB factsheet) for expanding capabilities. MTI says 5% or 10% depending on the investment committed.
  - Both sit under the Economic Expansion Incentives (Relief from Income Tax) Act.
  - Sources: MTI PQ reply https://www.mti.gov.sg/newsroom/written-reply-to-pq-on-pioneer-certificate-incentive/ ; EDB DEI factsheet https://www.edb.gov.sg/content/dam/edb-en/how-we-help/incentive-and-schemes/factsheets/DEI(Mfg)%20Factsheet.pdf
